Five words that explain the whole tool

  • Take: one result: a still or a clip. Every Generate makes one or more takes.
  • Shot: a group of takes of the same idea (shot 3, takes A, B, C).
  • Reel: a project, holding all its shots and takes. (On the Content Generator it's called a session.)
  • Board: a reel planned as a spot: one card per shot, in order, with a running time.
  • Kit: the cast, products and places that must look the same in every shot of a spot.

Make a film, Autopilot, the Lab and ComfyUI

The parts that make Gobo more than a prompt box. The Lab runs in Rust in your browser, and the same Rust grades films on the server.

Make a film: say it in one line

Gobo opens on Make a film. Write one line about your film (who, where, what happens), or press one of the ideas under the box. Then pick:

  • The look: Drama, Thriller, Sci-fi, Western, Horror, Neon night, Music video, Road trip, Documentary, Advert, Black & white or Home video. Each sets the genre the shots are written for, and the film stock and grade the finished film is graded through. Let Gobo decide leaves it to the shot list.
  • Length: 10, 15 or 30 seconds.
  • Shape: Wide for screens, Tall for phones (Reels, TikTok), Square for feeds.
  • Quality: Draft is the cheapest, for trying an idea (LTX 2.5 Fast); Standard is good and quick (LTX 2.5 Pro); Best uses the finest clip model, Seedance 2.5, and costs more. Each shot starts from a keyframe drawn by Nano Banana (Draft) or FLUX 3.

The line under Roll camera says what it should cost on your Comfy account. If the account can't cover it, Gobo says so before anything is spent. While it rolls, each step is shown in plain words with the frames as they land; it usually takes 5 to 10 minutes, and you can look around the studio meanwhile (the 🎬 Make a film button in the header comes back).

When the film is done it plays right there. Share makes a public page anyone with the link can watch, with no sign-in; Unshare takes it down. Change a shot opens the film on the Board, where every shot can be redone and the film cut again, and Regrade it opens the Lab. Your latest films are under the box when you come back.

Direct it yourself opens the full studio: every camera, lens and light control, every model, the Board and the Lab. Make a film is Autopilot with the choices made for you.

Autopilot: one line, a finished film

Click ▶ Autopilot in the header, write one line (who, where, what happens, and a look if you have one in mind), and click Roll camera. It does what you would do on the Board, in order, and you can watch each step land:

  • The shot list. Autopilot writes the shots, a title and their lengths.
  • Keyframes. Every new setup gets a still on the keyframe model, all at once.
  • Clips. Every shot is animated from its keyframe. A shot marked ↪ starts on the last frame of the one before, so the action carries across the cut.
  • The script supervisor. Every cut is checked in Rust: a straight cut must keep the grade, and a continuing shot must match the picture.
  • The cut, and then the grade: every frame goes through the shot's film stock and grade at the strength you picked (Off, Light, Medium or Full). Medium is the default, because the prompt already asked the model for the stock. Grading costs nothing.

The dialog shows what the run should cost before you start. A shot that fails is tried once more, then left out of the cut rather than stopping the film. A clip model that can't start from a frame runs every shot from its words, as straight cuts.

The Lab

Click Lab in the header. Everything in it runs in your browser, in Rust compiled to WebAssembly, so nothing is uploaded and nothing is charged.

  • Steal the look: drop in any frame. It reads the tone, colour, palette, grain, letterbox, focus and light, names the nearest film stock and grade, and sets them on the shot.
  • Grade: the selected take through a film stock and a grade, live, with the grain moving on video. Amount sets the strength; the other sliders adjust grain, halation, vignette, fade, contrast, colour and warmth. Drag the bar under the picture to compare with the original. Import LUT or CDL reads a .cube or a colourist's CDL into the sliders (see Colour), and Advanced opens Colour for wheels, curves and scopes. Grade the take grades every frame on the server and keeps the sound. The Lab closes on the new take, which says which frame it's on while it works (a clip takes a minute or two) and then plays the result; the original stays beside it in the strip. You can keep working meanwhile, or press Stop grading. Download .cube gives the same colour to Resolve, Premiere or Final Cut.
  • Grade other takes to match: an extension, another angle or a retake, graded so it cuts with this take. Tick the takes, from This reel or from anywhere in the Library (a take from another reel is copied into this one first, so they cut together); each is first matched to this take's colour (Gobo reads frames of both and works out a CDL that lines up their blacks, mid-tones, whites and saturation, in Rust), then given this grade, as a new take beside its original. It says how far apart each was before and after. Open a graded take in the Lab and it regrades from the take it came from, with its grade loaded, so a grade you've made can be matched to the rest later.
  • Continuity: how each take ends against how the next begins, with the take whose look sits furthest from the rest called out.
  • Draw the move: draw on the frame with the camera pen and the subject pen. A loop reads as an orbit and a line toward the subject as a push-in; the subject's path becomes the action. Shoot it re-renders the take on screen with that move (a still is animated from), as a new take beside the old one, on the clip model picked on the left (change the model there first to reshoot it on another); Just set it only puts the move and the action on the shot, for you to generate later.

Colour: wheels, curves, scopes and LUTs

The Lab's Grade is the simple version. Advanced: wheels, curves, scopes opens Colour, the take full screen with a colourist's tools, in the spirit of DaVinci Resolve's Color page. Both work on the same grade, so you can go back and forth (Simple), and Grade the take grades every frame on the server with everything you set.

  • Scopes: Waveform (brightness across the frame, 0 at the bottom, 100 at the top), Parade (red, green and blue side by side), Vectorscope (hue round the circle, saturation outward, with the skin-tone line and where full colours land) and Histogram. They read the graded picture as you change it.
  • Primaries: four wheels. Drag Lift toward a colour to tint the shadows, Gamma the mid-tones, Gain the highlights and Offset everything; the slider under each makes that range darker or brighter. Double-click a wheel to centre it.
  • Curves: Y bends all three channels, R, G and B one each. Click to add a point, drag it, double-click to take it out. The curve never overshoots its points.
  • LUT and CDL: Import LUT or CDL takes a .cube from Resolve, Premiere, Final Cut, a LUT pack or a camera maker (3D or 1D), or a colourist's ASC CDL (.cdl, .cc). A LUT is kept with the reel, so every take in it can use it.
    • A LUT is read into the sliders: exposure, contrast, fade, the white point, saturation, warmth, tint and the wheels move to the nearest they come to it. A .cube is a table of colours, not settings, so the sliders can't always say all of it; what they can't (one colour turned into another, say) is kept as a small LUT that goes before them, so the picture is exactly the LUT's. Move a slider and the picture moves from there. Amount sets how much of it.
    • Keep it as one LUT instead puts the sliders back and keeps the LUT whole, with its own amount, After the look (a creative LUT) or First (a camera's log-to-video LUT). Read it into the sliders goes the other way.
    • A LUT that no setting of the sliders comes near (a negative, a posterise, a swap of colours) stays whole, and one that barely changes the picture is simply on.
    • A CDL goes on the wheels: its slope, offset and power are the Lift, Gamma and Gain wheels exactly, and its saturation goes on Saturation.
  • Look, Basics and Film: the stock and grade, exposure, contrast and its pivot, saturation, warmth and tint; then grain, halation, vignette, fade, the white point and the grade's overall amount.

The order is the one a colourist uses: a camera LUT, the CDL, the wheels, the curves, the stock and grade, a creative LUT, then grain, halation and the vignette. Download .cube carries all of it except those last three, which need the whole picture.

Grade by the scopes, not your screen: on the waveform, keep the blacks just above 0 and the brightest whites just under 100; a neutral grey should sit in the middle of the vectorscope.

Open in ComfyUI

A take made on a model ComfyUI has a node for shows Open in ComfyUI. It downloads a zip with workflow_api.json, any keyframes the graph loads (they go in ComfyUI's input folder), and a short read-me. Autopilot's Open in ComfyUI exports the whole film as one graph, with every keyframe node wired into its clip. Drag the JSON onto ComfyUI, sign in to your Comfy account, and queue it.

A take's colour, in your own ComfyUI. Open in ComfyUI in the Lab's Grade (and on the Colour page) opens your ComfyUI with the take on screen and its look laid out as Gobo's own nodes:

  • Gobo Load Take fetches the take from Gobo by a link that works for a week, without signing in.
  • Gobo Look: the film stock and grade with every slider set as in the Lab (picking another stock fills the sliders with its numbers, as the Lab does); wheels, curves and a CDL ride in its colour box.
  • Gobo LUT for a LUT on the look, and Gobo Shot Match with the reference take, for a take matched to another.
  • Gobo Grade puts every frame through the look with the same Rust as Gobo (the grain moving, the sound kept), then Save Video.

Change anything there and queue it. The nodes are part of Gobo: the first time, Open in ComfyUI asks where your ComfyUI is (the desktop app is usually http://127.0.0.1:8000) and offers Get the Gobo nodes: unzip them into ComfyUI/custom_nodes, run pip install wasmtime in ComfyUI's Python and restart it. Shift-click Open in ComfyUI to change the address. There are more nodes to build with: Gobo Steal Look, Gobo Grade Images and Gobo Continuity.

Who can sign in

An admin (set on the server with STUDIO_ADMINS) has Users on the home page. There you add someone with a username and a password (Make one up writes a strong one), give someone a new password, or remove them. Send them the address, their username and their password: the password is shown once and kept only as a hash.

  • A new password, or removing someone, signs them out wherever they were. Their takes stay in the Library under their name.
  • A name with an account signs in only with its own password, never the studio's shared one. If you signed in with the shared password, Set my password gives you an account of your own.

Your Comfy key on the public demo

On the public demo you sign in with your own Comfy API key, and everything you make is charged to your Comfy account. Comfy checks the key, and Gobo holds it in memory while you work: never on disk, never in a cookie. Only you see your reels. Sign out forgets the key, in this browser too.

The basics

The page has three columns: settings on the left, the picture in the middle, the prompt on the right.

Finding your way around

  • Header: the reel's name (click it to rename), Library, Reels, New reel, this Guide, your Comfy balance, and Prompt to show or hide the right column.
  • Left panel: what to make (Still image or Video clip), the model, the scene, the camera and look, the format, and the Generate button at the bottom.
  • Middle: the Shot and Board switch, the stage where the current take plays, its actions, and the Sequence bar with every take in the reel.
  • Right: the prompt that will actually be sent, coloured by which control wrote each part, and details of the selected take.

Your first still

  1. At the top of the left panel, choose Still image.
  2. Click the model button and pick a model. Each one shows its price per image.
  3. In Scene, describe the moment: who, where, what is happening.
  4. Optionally set the camera, light and look under Shot, or leave them on Auto.
  5. Set how many takes you want (1–4) under Format, then click Generate image (or press ⌘↵).

Takes land in the Sequence bar, grouped by shot. Click one to see it on the stage.

Your first clip

  1. Choose Video clip at the top of the left panel (it's the default).
  2. Pick a video model. The button shows its length range, resolution, whether it makes sound, and its price.
  3. In Scene, describe the action and how the camera moves.
  4. Set Movement under Shot (push in, dolly, crane, orbit…), and the length under Format.
  5. Click Generate clip. Most clips take one to five minutes. Keep working; it lands when it's done.
To control the opening frame exactly, make a still first, then click Animate on it. The clip starts from that picture.

What things cost

Every model button and picker row shows its price (≈ $ per image or per clip; /s means per second). The cost line under Generate adds it up before you click. Your balance is in the header.

The bill is often lower than the listed price: some models charge by length or resolution. See Costs and timing for real examples.

Stop waiting stops the page watching a take; it does not stop the provider, so a stopped take may still be billed. Use Check again to collect it.

Directing a shot

Everything in the left panel, top to bottom.

Choosing a model

Click the model button to open the picker: every model of the current kind with its price and what it can do. Gobo's picks come first (under Comfy · Cinema, Fast drafts and so on), then the rest by maker.

  • Search by name or maker, or filter: for stills References, Text & type, Fast, Under $0.05; for video Start frame, End frame, Audio, 10s+, Change video, Extend video.
  • Comfy Router's catalogue comes with each release.
  • Under each name is a one-line verdict. Click the ? at the end of a row, or Model directory at the top, for what that model is good at and where it falls down: see the Model directory.

Shootout: one shot on several models

Click Shootout above the model button and tick up to three more models. Generate then runs the same shot on all of them, one take each, so you can compare.

Scene and prompt modes

The Scene is your words. Under it, three modes decide what else is added:

  • Plain: just your words and the direction you set.
  • Cinematic: adds "cinematic film still" (or motion, for video).
  • Director: the Director rewrites the whole brief for the model you picked. Click Direct it, read its version on the right, and edit it if you like.

To write the prompt yourself, turn on Edit in the Prompt column on the right.

Camera and look

The Shot list sets the cinematography. Click a row to see pictures of each option.

  • Camera: body, lens, focal length, aperture.
  • Framing and Angle: wide, medium, close-up…; eye level, low, overhead…
  • Movement (video only): push in, dolly, crane, orbit, handheld, drone…
  • Light, Film stock, Look and Grade. Grade can also be your own palette, picked by hand or taken from an image.
  • Composition: rule of thirds, centred, copy space…

Starter shots loads a ready-made shot, Shuffle tries a random direction and keeps your scene, All auto clears every control, and Reset goes back to the default look.

Format

  • Aspect (frame shape) and Resolution: only what the chosen model supports.
  • Takes (stills): 1–4 in the studio, up to 20 in the Content Generator. Each take is a different variation.
  • Duration and Audio (video): the lengths the model can make; sound only on models that make it.

Negative prompt and seed

Open Advanced under Format.

  • Negative prompt: what to keep out (text, watermark, extra fingers…). Only on models that take one.
  • Seed: leave it blank for a random result each time. Set a number (🎲 picks one) to repeat a result; several takes of one shot still differ.

References and frames

The slots under the Scene take pictures (or a clip) that the model uses. They change with the model:

  • Character / product: keeps a person or product looking the same. Models list how many references they take.
  • Starting frame (video): the clip starts on this picture.
  • Ending frame (video): the clip ends on this picture. It needs a starting frame too.
  • Video to edit: for models that change or extend a clip.

Click a slot to upload, or drag a take from the Sequence bar onto it.

Working with takes

What happens after you click Generate.

While it renders, and when it fails

  • A rendering take shows a timer. You can keep directing, switch reels, even close the tab: it keeps going and lands in the Sequence bar.
  • Stop waiting stops watching it. Check again on a stopped take asks the provider once more and collects it if it finished.
  • A failed take says why, with Try again, Try on … (another model) and Edit scene.
  • A failure stays on screen until you dismiss it: in a red panel (bottom right; top on a phone) and on its Board card, saying in plain English what went wrong and what to try. What the model said shows the model's own words; Show me goes to the card or take; Got it clears it. Click a red letter on a card to read it again.
  • A run the Studio refuses before sending (a stretch too long for the tool, say) goes in the same panel, so nothing is spent and the reason stays put.
  • In line: each maker runs only so many clips at once (Luma, four), so when you animate a whole Board the rest wait their turn and start by themselves. A waiting card says In line · 2 ahead; Don't run it takes it out of the line and nothing is spent. A maker that says it's busy gets the take back in line, not a failure.
  • The balance in the header (your Comfy credits) is live: it updates as takes land, every minute, and when you click it. ≈$10.00 in progress is what's rendering or waiting in line. It turns amber with a top up link when what's left after that is under $5, and a run the account can't pay for is refused before it's sent.

Comparing, zooming, starring

  • The letters on the stage (A, B, C) switch between takes of the same shot.
  • Compare with… puts another take beside this one with a slider.
  • Zoom (stills): click the picture where you want to look closer.
  • ★ stars a take (or press S). Starred takes can be downloaded on their own and filtered in the Library.

What you can do with a still

  • Generate again: same settings, new take. Reuse settings: load them into the panel to change something.
  • Use as reference: make it a character or product reference.
  • Animate: make it the starting frame of a clip. Use as end frame: the ending frame.
  • Upscale: twice the size. Remove background: the subject on transparency.
  • Copy prompt, Delete.

What you can do with a clip

  • Continue shot: the next clip starts on this clip's last frame. Describe what happens next.
  • Grab frame: pause the clip where you want, then pull that frame out as a still.
  • Change clip, Extend clip, Lead into it: see your own footage; they work on any clip.
  • Upscale: a sharper, larger clip.

The Sequence bar: select, stitch, delete

  • Select (or ⌘/Shift-click a take) starts a selection. Click takes to pick them; Select all picks everything.
  • Stitch picks one take per shot (the starred one, else a clip, else the newest) and joins them in order into one video. Stills are held for 2, 3, 5 or 8 seconds (Stills hold).
  • With a selection: Download it as a zip, or Delete it (or press Delete). A take still rendering can't be deleted; stop it first.
  • Done or Esc leaves selecting.
  • A take that failed (✕) or that you stopped (■) is a narrow sliver beside the ones that worked: hover for why, click it for Try again, Try on another model, or Edit scene. Clear N failed takes them all out of the reel at once.

Downloads

  • All takes (.zip) or Starred (.zip): separate files in one zip, with a list of what each is.
  • Download stitched: the last stitched film. Any take also has Download on the stage.
  • Content Generator: pick Original, 2K, 4K or 8K at 300 dpi before downloading; stills are resized and sharpened for filing.

Share a take

Under any finished still or clip, Share makes a public page for it and copies the link: anyone who has the link can watch it and download it, without signing in, and nothing else of yours is visible. The page isn't listed in search engines. Press Copy share link to copy it again, or Unshare to take it down; the link stops working at once.

Reels and the Library

Nothing you make is lost until you delete it.

Reels

  • New reel starts a fresh one; your first Generate creates it. Click the name in the header to rename it.
  • Reels lists them all, newest first, with a cover picture. Click one to open it.
  • Takes stay until you delete them. Only reels with nothing made in them are cleared, after 60 days.

The Library

Library shows every finished take from every reel (or every session, on the Content Generator), grouped by day.

  • Mine is what you made; Everyone is the whole team's.
  • Filter by Clips or Stills, ★ Starred, or search prompts, models and reel names.
  • Hover over a clip to play it. Click any take to open the viewer; ← → move through the library, S stars, Esc goes back.

What the Library can do

  • On each take, under the pointer: On its board and As a clip, the same two ways in as the viewer's, without opening it first.
  • In the viewer: Open on its board (for a take made for a card on a board: it opens that board with the card using this take, so a film whose page was closed can still be cut; if the card was using another take, it says which, and clicking that letter on the card puts it back), Open as a clip (or a still: its reel, in the Shot view, with the take on the stage to play, grade or upscale), Download, Star, Copy prompt, Delete, and for stills Use as reference (or Add to kit when the Board is open), which brings the picture into the reel you're working in.
  • Several at once: tick takes (or ⌘-click). Download makes one zip with a folder per reel; Delete removes them.
  • Put on a board: the ticked takes become cards on a board, a new board or the one on screen, a clip as the card's clip and a still as its keyframe. They go in film order (the order of the board they were made for, else the order they were made), and a take from another reel is copied in, so the board has everything its cut needs. From there, Cut the spot, reorder, re-animate or extend them.
  • You can delete your own takes and ones with no name on them, not someone else's.

Why is "Mine" empty?

Takes are kept under the name you signed in with. Takes made before 23 October 2026 carry no name, so they show only under Everyone.

  1. Switch to Everyone.
  2. Tick your takes (Select all helps).
  3. Click Mark … as mine. A take with someone's name on it keeps it.

If the Library says it doesn't know who you are, sign in again from the home page.

The Board: plan a TV spot

Click Board above the stage. A spot is a fixed pipeline: brief → shot list → keyframes → clips → the cut. Each shot is a card.

A spot in seven steps

  1. Put the cast and product in the Kit.
  2. Set the look in the left panel (camera, light, film, grade) and pick a Keyframes model and a Clips model.
  3. Write the brief, pick :15, :30 or :60, and click Write the shot list.
  4. Read the cards and fix what you'd change.
  5. Keyframe every card (cents each) and click Animatic to check the timing.
  6. Animate the cards you're happy with.
  7. Trim, then Cut the spot.

From a brief to a shot list

Write the brief at the top of the Board: the product, who it's for, the idea, the one thing to remember. Pick the length and click Write the shot list.

Gobo drafts the cards in about 15 seconds: what the camera sees, framing, camera move, length, who's in each shot, a voice-over line and on-screen text. The lengths are ones the Clips model can actually make, and they add up to the length you picked.

Rewrite the shot list replaces the AI cards. Your footage cards stay, and Gobo plans around them.

The Kit: the same face, the same product, every shot

The Kit (left panel, on the Board) holds the people, products and places that must look the same throughout. Each is one good picture with a name.

  • Upload a picture, From library (open a still, then Add to kit), or drag a keyframe from a card onto the Kit.
  • Name it ("Maya", "the Icebox bottle") and set it to Cast, Product or Place.
  • On each card, tick who and what is in that shot. Their pictures go to the keyframe, and the prompt names them ("image 1 is Maya").
Pick a Keyframes model that takes several references: Nano Banana Pro, GPT Image or Seedream. The keyframe carries the kit into the clip.

Spot seed and the look

  • The Spot seed (in the Kit) gives every shot its own fixed seed, so a retake of a shot repeats it. Faces and products come from the kit, not the seed.
  • On the Board, the left panel is the spot's look: camera, light, film, look and grade apply to every card. Framing, movement and length are set on each card.

Keyframes and Clips models

On the Board the model card has two rows: Keyframes (a still model) and Clips (a video model). Click either to choose.

For Clips, pick a model that makes video from a keyframe (Seedance, Veo, Kling…). Models that change or extend a clip belong on footage cards; as the Clips model, AI shots fail on them.

A card, field by field

  • Number and length at the top, with Cut / Continue, and ← → to move it a place (or drag the card by its top bar, ⠿, to anywhere in the film: a line shows where it lands; Esc puts it back), ⧉ to duplicate, ✕ to take it off the board (its takes stay in the reel).
  • The picture: the chosen clip, else the keyframe. Hover over it to play. Open ↗ opens that take in the Shot view, for upscaling, grabbing frames and the rest.
  • Key and Clip letters: every keyframe and clip made for this card. Click one to choose it.
  • What the camera sees, then Framing, Move and Length.
  • Kit chips: tick who and what is in the shot.
  • Voice-over and On-screen text: saved with the spot for the edit.

Keyframes

Keyframe makes the opening picture of the shot with the Keyframes model and the kit. Keyframes per shot (top of the Board) makes 1–4 options at once; click a letter to choose one. New keyframe makes more; the newest is shown until you pick another.

Animate, and ending on the next shot

  • Animate makes the clip from the chosen keyframe with the Clips model, at the card's length and move.
  • Tick ends on N (on models that can end on a frame) and the clip finishes exactly on the next card's keyframe: a seamless hand-off.
  • Re-animate makes another version; choose between them with the Clip letters.

Continue: picking up where the last shot ends

Set a card to Continue and its keyframe is the last frame of the previous card's clip. Animate the previous card first, then click Last frame of N, then Animate.

Trims and running time

  • Every card with a clip has In and Out (bottom right). Type the seconds to use only that stretch.
  • The timeline under the brief shows each card's length: grey for a keyframe, amber for a clip, blue for footage. The red line is your target, and it says how far over or short you are.
  • Click a block in the timeline to jump to its card.

Keyframe all, Animate all

The buttons under the timeline do every card that's ready, and show the total cost first: Keyframe N shots for cards with no keyframe, Animate N shots for cards with a keyframe and no clip. Animate asks before it spends.

Continue cards wait for the clip before them; read their last frame once it lands.

Animatic and Cut the spot

  • Animatic: the keyframes held for each card's length, with footage as it is. Check the timing before paying for video.
  • Cut the spot: the chosen clips, trimmed, in board order. A keyframe stands in where a card has no clip yet.
  • The ▶ button beside them (for example ▶ Spot cut · 0:12.5) plays the last cut in the Shot view, with Download on the stage.

Your own footage

Bring in a clip you shot and work it with AI: restyle it, swap in a product, extend it, lead into it, cut it with AI shots.

Getting footage in

  • On the Board: click ＋ Footage at the end of the cards. In the Shot view: ＋ Footage in the Sequence bar.
  • MP4, MOV or WebM, up to 100 MB. Export a 1080p H.264 clip; 30 seconds is about 40–60 MB. (QuickTime: File → Export As → 1080p.)
  • The original is kept. The page plays (and the models get) a 1080p copy; 50 and 60 fps clips come in at 25 and 30.
Use the clean master, not a watermarked comp: the AI copies the watermark into what it makes.

A footage card

A footage card has a blue border and a Footage tag. It shows the clip and its first frame (the Key), then:

  • What's in this footage: one line; Gobo plans the AI shots around it.
  • The tool: a Change it or Extend it model, with its price.
  • The instruction: the change, or what happens next.
  • In and Out: the stretch to work on. Changes and extensions run on just that part; most models work best on 3–8 seconds.

Change it: restyle, or swap in a product

  • Restyle the same take: keep FLUX 3 Video Edit (or Luma Modify, Runway Aleph, Kling O3 Edit), type the change ("make it a snowy winter evening"), click Change. The motion and the people stay.
  • Swap in a product or a shirt:
    1. Put a clean picture of it in the Kit and name it the way you'll write it ("the olive blouse").
    2. Tick it on the footage card. (Name it in the instruction and it's sent anyway.)
    3. Choose a tool that shows refs: Pruna P Video Edit (≈ $0.41) first, then Kling O1 Video 2 Video Edit (≈ $0.93) or Kling O3 Video Edit (≈ $2.09).
    4. Say what changes and what stays: "Replace her western shirt with the olive blouse. Keep her face, hair, hat, earrings, movement and the background exactly the same."
    5. Set In and Out to 3–5 seconds and click Change.

Pruna P Video Replace swaps a whole person for the one in the kit picture, and takes no instruction. Tools without "refs" (FLUX 3 Video Edit, Wan 2.7) can't see the kit; they go by your words alone.

Some tools take a set length: Kling O3 takes 3–15 seconds and is told the stretch's length. The card stops you if the stretch is longer than a tool takes.

Each result is a new version on the same card (B, C…). Click the letters to compare with the original.

Extend it

Choose an Extend it tool (FLUX 3 Extend, PixVerse V6 Extend), set how much longer (+5s…), say what happens next, and click Extend. The extension becomes the next card and carries on from the Out point, camera move and all. Only the new seconds play; the original isn't repeated.

Lead into it with an AI shot

  1. Click ＋ AI shot and move it before the footage with ←.
  2. Describe the opening ("aerial of the street at golden hour, coming down toward her") and click Keyframe.
  3. Tick ends on N and click Animate. The AI shot lands exactly on the footage's first frame (on the version the footage card shows, if you've changed it).

To follow the footage with an AI shot instead, add a card after it set to Continue.

Let Gobo plan around it

With footage cards on the Board, Write the shot list keeps each piece at its real length and writes AI shots around it that match its people, place and light.

To keep the talent in the AI shots, click Key on the footage card to show its first frame, drag it onto the Kit and name the person. Tick them on the AI cards.

Footage in the Shot view

Every clip on the stage, yours or AI, has Change clip, Extend clip and Lead into it. They load the right kind of model and the clip, then you describe the change and click Generate. The Shot view has no In/Out: it sends the whole clip. Use a footage card on the Board to work on part of one.

Which tools to use, and the rules

  • Google's video models refuse real people. They sort last in the tool list and say "no real people". Use the others for footage with people in it.
  • Seedance refuses a starting frame of a realistic person. ByteDance blocks any keyframe or reference that looks like a real face, so a shot of people that starts from a keyframe comes back refused. Animate those on MiniMax, Wan or Kling.
  • Releases: before AI-changed footage of real talent goes to a client, make sure the model releases cover AI changes to their likeness.

The Content Generator

Batches of stills

Every still model is in the picker with its price. Set up to 20 Images under Format and click Generate. The camera and look controls are there whenever you want to direct a shot.

Prompts from a reference picture

  1. In From a reference, drop an image or click Image.
  2. Choose Photo or Illustration, how many Prompts, and the Variation (1 = close to the picture, 10 = loose).
  3. Add art direction for every prompt if you like ("diverse cast, winter light, no text").
  4. Click Write N prompts. GPT-4o writes them in a few seconds.
  5. Edit or drop any (✕), Use one as the Scene, or generate them all at once.

A whole folder

Click Folder and pick a folder of pictures. Run N images × M writes prompts for each picture and generates them, at most 8 at a time. Stop finishes the current picture and stops.
